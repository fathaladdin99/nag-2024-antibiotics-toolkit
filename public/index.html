🛡️ Penicillin Allergy Decision Guide

Appendix 3

Based on NAG 2024 Appendix 3: Protocol for evaluating penicillin allergies to safely navigate cephalosporin and carbapenem use.

1. Immediate / Severe Type 1 IgE Reaction?

Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, angioedema, hypotension within 1 hour of dose, or severe cutaneous reactions (SCAR / SJS / TEN / DRESS).

🚫 AVOID: All Penicillins and 1st/2nd Gen Cephalosporins.
✅ SAFE ALTERNATIVES: Macrolides (Azithromycin, Erythromycin), Glycopeptides (Vancomycin), Clindamycin, Aminoglycosides, Ciprofloxacin.
2. Non-Severe / Delayed Cutaneous Rash?

Isolated maculopapular rash developing > 72 hours after initiation, without mucosal involvement or systemic symptoms.

⚠️ Cephalosporin Cross-Reactivity is < 1-2%: 3rd and 4th generation cephalosporins (Cefotaxime, Ceftriaxone, Ceftazidime) and Carbapenems (Meropenem) have different side chains and can generally be safely administered under medical observation.

🔄 IV to Oral Switch Criteria (Appendix 6)

Appendix 6

Early conversion from IV to oral therapy reduces cannula-related bloodstream infections, length of stay, and costs. Consider switch when all 4 criteria are fulfilled:

✅ 1. Clinical Improvement

Patient is hemodynamically stable, vital signs normalizing, resolving tachypnoea and respiratory distress.

✅ 2. Afebrile for 24-48 Hours

Temperature < 38.0°C consistently without requiring frequent antipyretics.

✅ 3. Intact Oral Route

Child is alert, tolerating oral fluids/feeds, and not actively vomiting or with ileus/malabsorption.

✅ 4. Oral Option Available

Suitable oral bioequivalent formulation exists (e.g. Cefuroxime IV → Cefuroxime Axetil PO; Ampicillin IV → Amoxicillin PO; Cloxacillin IV → Cephalexin PO).
